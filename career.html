Accountant Skills, By Career Stage

What matters most changes as you move from fresher to senior accountant.

Fresher / 0-2 Years

Journal entries, trial balance, bank reconciliation, GST/TDS basics, Excel fluency, and comfort with one accounting software.

Mid-Level / 2-5 Years

Finalization of accounts, GST/TDS return filing ownership, MIS reporting, reconciliations across multiple accounts, and mentoring juniors.

Senior / 5+ Years

Process ownership (full R2R or P2P cycle), audit coordination, financial reporting, and cross-team stakeholder communication.

Resume Tips for Accountants

Small changes that make a real difference to how recruiters read your resume.

Do

  • Name specific tools: "Tally, Excel (VLOOKUP/Pivot Tables), GST portal"
  • Describe actual tasks from internships/articleship, not just the firm name
  • Quantify where you can — "reconciled 200+ vendor accounts monthly"
  • Keep it to one page as a fresher

Avoid

  • Vague phrases like "good with numbers" or "hard-working team player"
  • Listing every software you've ever opened once
  • Dense paragraphs — recruiters skim resumes in seconds
  • Unverifiable claims about outcomes you didn't directly own

How Accountants Grow Their Salary

Compensation growth in accounting tends to follow specific, demonstrable skill upgrades rather than time alone.

Build Compliance Depth

Owning GST or TDS return filing end-to-end, not just data entry, is a common first step up.

Add Reporting Skills

MIS reporting and variance analysis make you useful beyond bookkeeping.

Learn a Process Cycle Deeply

Owning a full R2R, P2P or O2C process signals readiness for a senior or team-lead role.

Develop Stakeholder Communication

Being able to explain numbers to non-finance stakeholders opens the door to analyst and manager-track roles.
