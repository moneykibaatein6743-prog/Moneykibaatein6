# Journal Entries – Practical Accounting Guide

This section contains practical journal entries commonly used by accountants in day-to-day accounting.

## 1. Capital Introduced

Example:

Owner introduced ₹1,00,000 into the business through bank.

### Journal Entry

Bank A/c Dr. ₹1,00,000
    To Capital A/c ₹1,00,000

---

## 2. Purchase of Goods for Cash

Example:

Goods purchased for ₹20,000 in cash.

### Journal Entry

Purchase A/c Dr. ₹20,000
    To Cash A/c ₹20,000

---

## 3. Purchase of Goods on Credit

Example:

Goods purchased from ABC Traders for ₹50,000 on credit.

### Journal Entry

Purchase A/c Dr. ₹50,000
    To ABC Traders A/c ₹50,000

---

## 4. Sales on Credit

Example:

Goods sold to XYZ Ltd. for ₹75,000 on credit.

### Journal Entry

XYZ Ltd. A/c Dr. ₹75,000
    To Sales A/c ₹75,000

---

## 5. Salary Paid

Example:

Salary of ₹30,000 paid through bank.

### Journal Entry

Salary A/c Dr. ₹30,000
    To Bank A/c ₹30,000

---

## 6. Rent Paid

Example:

Office rent of ₹15,000 paid through bank.

### Journal Entry

Rent A/c Dr. ₹15,000
    To Bank A/c ₹15,000

---

## 7. Depreciation

Example:

Depreciation charged on office equipment ₹10,000.

### Journal Entry

Depreciation A/c Dr. ₹10,000
    To Office Equipment A/c ₹10,000

---

## 8. Customer Payment Received

Example:

₹40,000 received from XYZ Ltd. through bank.

### Journal Entry

Bank A/c Dr. ₹40,000
    To XYZ Ltd. A/c ₹40,000

---

## 9. Vendor Payment

Example:

₹25,000 paid to ABC Traders through bank.

### Journal Entry

ABC Traders A/c Dr. ₹25,000
    To Bank A/c ₹25,000

---

## 10. Practical Learning

Journal entries are the foundation of bookkeeping and financial accounting.

An accountant should understand:

- Nature of accounts
- Debit and credit rules
- Transaction analysis
- GST impact
- TDS impact
- Ledger posting
- Trial balance
- Finalization
