# Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)

## What is BRS?

A Bank Reconciliation Statement is prepared to reconcile the balance as per the company's books with the balance shown in the bank statement.

## Why is BRS Important?

BRS helps an accountant to:

- Identify missing transactions
- Find bank charges
- Identify cheque differences
- Check direct bank credits
- Identify errors
- Keep bank balances accurate

## Common Reasons for Differences

### 1. Cheque Issued but Not Presented

A cheque may be recorded in the books but not yet presented to the bank.

### 2. Cheque Deposited but Not Cleared

A cheque may be recorded in the books but the bank may not have cleared it yet.

### 3. Bank Charges

The bank may deduct charges directly from the account.

### 4. Interest Credited by Bank

The bank may credit interest directly into the account.

### 5. Direct Customer Payment

A customer may directly deposit money into the company's bank account.

### 6. Standing Instructions

The bank may automatically make payments such as insurance, loan EMI, or other charges.

## Practical Example

Balance as per Cash Book: ₹1,00,000

Add:

Interest credited by bank: ₹2,000

Less:

Bank charges not recorded in books: ₹500

### Adjusted Balance

₹1,01,500

## Accountant's Checklist

- Match opening balance
- Compare bank transactions with books
- Check all deposits
- Check all withdrawals
- Identify bank charges
- Check direct credits
- Check outstanding cheques
- Check uncleared deposits
- Pass required adjustment entries
- Finalize reconciliation

## Career Relevance

BRS is an important practical skill for accountants because it helps maintain accurate bank and cash records and supports month-end closing and financial reporting.
