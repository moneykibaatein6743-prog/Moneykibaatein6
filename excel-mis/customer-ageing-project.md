# Customer Ageing Analysis Project

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates a practical Customer Ageing Analysis process used by accountants and Accounts Receivable (AR) teams.

The project uses fictional data for learning and portfolio purposes.

## Objective

The objective is to analyse outstanding customer invoices based on the number of days they have remained unpaid.

## Ageing Categories

Common ageing buckets include:

* Current / Not Due
* 1–30 Days
* 31–60 Days
* 61–90 Days
* 91–180 Days
* Above 180 Days

## Practical Workflow

### Step 1 – Collect Customer Data

Obtain the customer ledger or outstanding invoice report from the accounting system.

### Step 2 – Check Invoice Details

Review:

* Customer name
* Invoice number
* Invoice date
* Due date
* Invoice amount
* Amount received
* Outstanding amount

### Step 3 – Calculate Outstanding Days

Calculate the number of days between the due date and the reporting date.

### Step 4 – Classify Ageing

Place each outstanding invoice into the appropriate ageing bucket.

### Step 5 – Analyse Customer Outstanding

Identify:

* High-value outstanding invoices
* Overdue invoices
* Long-pending balances
* Customers requiring follow-up

### Step 6 – Prepare MIS

Prepare a summary showing:

* Total outstanding
* Current outstanding
* 1–30 days
* 31–60 days
* 61–90 days
* 91–180 days
* Above 180 days

## Business Use

Ageing analysis helps finance teams monitor receivables and support collection follow-up.

It is commonly relevant to:

* Accounts Receivable
* Order to Cash (O2C)
* Credit Control
* Finance Operations
* Corporate Accounting
