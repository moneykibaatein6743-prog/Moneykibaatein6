# Vendor Reconciliation Project

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates a practical Vendor Reconciliation process used by accountants to compare vendor ledger balances with vendor statements.

The project uses fictional data for learning and portfolio purposes.

## Objective

The objective is to identify differences between:

**Company Books → Vendor Ledger → Vendor Statement → Reconciliation**

## Skills Demonstrated

* Vendor Reconciliation
* Accounts Payable (AP)
* Ledger Review
* Invoice Matching
* Payment Matching
* Outstanding Payables
* Debit/Credit Difference Analysis
* Excel Data Analysis
* Accounting Review

## Common Reconciliation Differences

An accountant may identify differences due to:

* Invoice recorded in books but missing in vendor statement
* Invoice appearing in vendor statement but missing in books
* Payment recorded in books but not reflected by vendor
* Payment received/adjusted by vendor but not recorded in books
* Credit Note differences
* Debit Note differences
* TDS deducted but not adjusted in vendor statement
* Duplicate invoices
* Incorrect invoice amount
* Opening balance differences

## Practical Workflow

### Step 1 – Collect Vendor Ledger

Download or extract the vendor ledger from the accounting system.

### Step 2 – Obtain Vendor Statement

Obtain the statement shared by the vendor.

### Step 3 – Match Transactions

Compare:

* Invoice number
* Invoice date
* Invoice amount
* Payment details
* Credit notes
* Debit notes
* TDS
* Closing balance

### Step 4 – Identify Differences

Prepare a reconciliation sheet showing matched and unmatched transactions.

### Step 5 – Investigate Differences

Check supporting documents such as:

* Purchase invoices
* Payment entries
* Bank statements
* Credit notes
* Debit notes
* TDS records
* Email confirmations

### Step 6 – Pass Corrections

Where required, record the appropriate accounting adjustment after proper verification and authorization.

### Step 7 – Finalize Reconciliation

Confirm that the company ledger and vendor balance are properly reconciled.
